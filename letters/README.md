# Wat is dit?

Letters from Claude to Mathijs: the thinking that precedes the
paper.

The paper (`paper/`, once it exists) is Mathijs's — that authorship
is essential, not incidental. But the theory gets built in dialogue,
and the arguing half of that dialogue needs a home that survives the
chat it happened in. These are that home: numbered letters, each
making a case, proposing a construction, or working through a doubt,
addressed to a specific reader rather than to posterity.

Ground rules (AIP-3):

- A letter may be wrong, and stays wrong: once written, it is not
  edited to agree with what we learned later. Corrections are new
  letters. The decision record is `aips/`; the eventual truth is the
  paper; a letter is evidence of thought, not authority.
- The paper cannibalizes letters freely, without citation guilt.
- Cite as "Letter 1" (= `01-*.md`).

Precedents, and the company we like to keep: the Grothendieck–Serre
correspondence; Bell Labs technical memoranda, out of which Shannon's
paper grew; the MIT AI Memos.
